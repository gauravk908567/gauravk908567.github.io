Production

How it was run

Three people, each with a clear lane, and a level planned in four parts.

  • Me. Production, the art direction, the camera, the environment and level design, the shaders, the gameplay video and improvements to the player combat.

  • The programmer. Most of the AI code and the player code.

  • The sound designer. I wrote the list of sounds and talked through how each should feel. The sounds came back to me, I put them in the game, and we tested and approved each one together.

The look of the game

Decision
I set the art direction, so every area, shader and effect pulled toward one look.

Where the enemies go

Decision
Basic enemies at the start, and more kinds of enemy the further the player goes, so each new type arrives once the player is ready for it.

A terrain too big for the AI

Problem
The terrain was so large that the enemies crashed the game.
What we did
We split the terrain into smaller areas in code and gave the AI our own grid zones instead of Unity NavMesh.
Result
Each zone knows where an enemy can walk and when its enemies spawn and despawn, so the game holds together.

How the grid zones work

  • The terrain data is read and laid out as a grid
  • Each cell is marked: walkable or not
  • The grid for each area is saved as a JSON file and loaded with that area
  • Enemies decide where to walk from their zone, not from the whole terrain
  • Spawning and despawning run per zone, so only the enemies near the player are alive

Why it stopped

The game needed far more art than a team of three could make, and a teammate had to step away. We stopped after the first area.

Story

The world it is set in

A careless king raises taxes until his village runs out of resources. A rival king, who prays to the devil, offers him endless wealth if he breaks the ancient sword his palace protects. The rival's people move in, their tainted supplies corrupt the villagers, and the protagonist, halfway to being turned himself, sets out to end the plan before it finishes him.

The king

The village suffers from the decisions of a careless king who cares for his wealth and ignores his people. He raises the taxes, crime grows, and when the village starts to run out of resources he grows angry, refuses to hear the poor and orders them to work with whatever they can find.

The deal

An evil king who prays to the devil notices. He offers endless wealth and resources for the villagers, on one condition: the king must break the ancient sword his palace keeps safe and let the evil king's people live among his own.

The sword

The king breaks the sword into four pieces, ties them to horses and sends them off in four directions. The evil king's people settle among the villagers, and their supplies are always full.

The corruption

The villagers are wary, but with no other choice they use those supplies. The tainted resources corrupt everything, and just as the evil king planned, the villagers begin to turn into his own kind.

The protagonist

Halfway through being turned, he decides to end the evil king's plan himself before it finishes him.
